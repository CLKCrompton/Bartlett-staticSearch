Timeline of the Editions

Fitting to an editions project, contextualizing Familiar Quotations can be aided by an outline of the 120-year publication history of the nineteen editions that form the Familiar Quotations corpus so far.I

1855II

First Edition

A Collection of Familiar Quotations, With Complete Indices of Authors and Subjects.

  • 295 pages.
  • While Bartlett’s name appeared as the publisher, he published the text with no author attribution.
  • Bartlett produced 1000 copies of this first edition, which sold out in three months (Cochrane, 1992).

1856

Second Edition

A Collection of Familiar Quotations, With Complete Indices of Authors and Subjects. Revised Edition.

  • 358 pages.

1858

Third EditionIII

A Collection of Familiar Quotations, With Complete Indices of Authors and Subjects. Third Edition, with Supplement.

  • 446 pages.
  • Bartlett’s initials are added to the preface.

1863

Fourth EditionIV

Familiar Quotations: Being an Attempt to Trace to Their Source Passages and Phrases in Common Use: Chiefly from English Authors

  • 480 pages.
  • With a Copious Verbal Index.
  • Title change, denoting the collection’s emphasis on English authors.
  • Published with an epigraph on the frontispiece: ‘I have gathered a posie of other men’s flowers, and nothing but the thread that binds them is mine own. - Montaigne.’
  • With Little, Brown and Company appearing as the publisher, the first versions of the fourth edition did not bear Bartlett’s name on the title page.
  • By 1865, however, Bartlett’s name begins appearing under the title as an author on the fourth edition (Cohen, 2003, p. 64).

1868V

Fifth Edition

Familiar Quotations: Being an Attempt to Trace to Their Source Passages and Phrases in Common Use. By John Bartlett.

  • 778 pages.
  • Title change, no longer emphasizing English authors.
  • Montaigne epigraph moved to the title page.

1874

Sixth Edition

Familiar Quotations: Being an Attempt to Trace to Their Source Passages and Phrases in Common Use. By John Bartlett.

  • 778 pages.

1875

Seventh Edition

Familiar Quotations: Being an Attempt to Trace to Their Source Passages and Phrases in Common Use. By John Bartlett.

  • 864 pages.

1882

Eighth Edition

Familiar Quotations: Being an Attempt to Trace to Their Source Passages and Phrases in Common Use. By John Bartlett.

  • 904 pages.

1891VI

Ninth EditionVII

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • 1,158 pages.
  • This is the last edition edited and authored by Bartlett.
  • Title change to indicate inclusion of Ancient and Modern literature and no longer framed as an “Attempt”.
  • Bartlett’s preface implies he did not expect future editions to be published: “The small thin volume, the first to bear the title of this collection, after passing through eight editions, each enlarged, now culminates in its ninth,—and with it, closes its tentative life.” (Bartlett 1891).

1914

Tenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • 1,454 pages.
  • First edition with editorial credit given to someone other than Bartlett.
  • Montaigne epigraph removed from the title page.

1937

Eleventh Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Edited by Christopher Morley and Louella D. Everett.
  • 1,578 pages.
  • The dust jacket reads Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations, while the title page officially says Familiar Quotations.
  • First edition to remove previously included quotations, a standard practice for all subsequent editions.(Cochrane, 1992)

1948

Twelfth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Edited by Christopher Morley and Louella D. Everett.
  • 1,831 pages.
  • Increase in contemporary authors and entrance of contemporary politics into the contents.(Cochrane, 1992)

1955

Thirteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations by John Bartlett: A Collection of Passages, Phrases and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature

  • Thirteenth and Centennial Edition; Completely Revised.
  • 1,614 pages.
  • Internally edited by Little, Brown and Company.

1968

Fourteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations by John Bartlett: A Collection of Passages, Phrases and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature

  • Revised and Enlarged.
  • Emily Morison Beck, Editor.
  • 1,749 pages.
  • First edition to be indexed by a computer.(Cochrane, 1992)

1980

Fifteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Fifteenth and 125th Anniversary Edition.
  • Revised and Enlarged.
  • Edited by Emily Morison Beck and the editorial staff of Little, Brown and Company.
  • 1,540 pages.

1992

Sixteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Justin Kaplan, General Editor.
  • 1,405 pages.

1995

Expanded Multimedia Edition

  • Version 1995, CD ROM.
  • Described by (Smith) as “a combination of Justin Kaplan’s 16th edition of Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations (NY: Little, Brown and Company 1992), Quick Time, Learn Technologies Interactive, and photographs, art, and film clips from the Archive Photos, Art Resource, Bettman Archive, and The WPA Film Library” (1996, p. 418).

2002

Seventeenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Justin Kaplan, General Editor.
  • 1,488 pages.
  • First edition where the dust jacket, cover, and title page all bear the title Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations.

2012

Eighteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Geoffrey O’Brien, General Editor.
  • 1,504 pages.
  • Released with a companion app, Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations Version 1.0 released 3 November 2012 by Hachette Book Group. Discussion of the indexing used to create the app can be found in (Maislin (2013)).
  • First Ebook edition released in December 2014.

2022

Nineteenth Edition

Familiar Quotations: A Collection of Passages, Phrases, and Proverbs Traced to Their Sources in Ancient and Modern Literature. By John Bartlett.

  • Geoffrey O’Brien, General Editor.
  • 1,504 pages.

Footnotes

I: For a detailed analysis of the paratextual changes that occurred across this publication and edition history, particularly in the nineteenth century, see (Cohen, 2003).

II: Around 1855, H. M. Caldwell published what Cohen refers to as a “slightly expanded (and likely illegal)” version of Bartlett’s text (Cohen, 2003, p. 57). It contains 316 pages and was published without a date.

III: The first three editions were self-published by John Bartlett (1820-1905) through the University Bookstore that he owned in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

IV: From the fourth edition onward, Familiar Quotations has been published by Little, Brown and Company which operated independently from 1837-1968. In 1968, they were bought by Time Inc. (L.A. Times, 1989) and in 2006, were acquired by Hachette UK, under which company the imprint continues to run (Little, Brown Book Group, 2019).

V: Before the regular edition print of the 5th edition, 100 limited edition copies were issued in a larger binding by Little, Brown and Company (Cohen, 2003, p. 57).

VI: In the 1890s, Cohen notes that “Hurst & Company of New York issued (probably illegally) a facsimile edition of Caldwell’s 1855 edition” (2003, p. 58)

VII: In 1898, Little, Brown and Company reprinted the ninth edition in a larger size.
