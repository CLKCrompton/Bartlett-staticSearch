About the Project

What does it mean for knowledge to be “familiar”? Bartlett’s Familiar Quotations (BFQ), first published in 1855, has been a cornerstone of reference literature, offering readers a curated collection of phrases and passages deemed essential to an educated public. Initially compiled by John Bartlett, the work offered the origins of literary lines that had entered common usage, reflecting the intellectual and cultural priorities of its time. A tremendously popular volume, over its first ten editions, BFQ evolved into a cultural touchstone, simultaneously preserving and shaping the canon of works with whom the culturally literate person should be familiar.

This project explores the overarching question: over the timespan of the first ten editions, what kind of material was supposed to be “familiar” to an educated public? The research examines how editorial decisions, societal norms, and historical contexts influenced the selection of authors and texts, uncovering patterns in gender, geography, and occupation. By investigating these dynamics, the research sheds light on the hierarchies and biases embedded in the construction of cultural memory. For a deeper understanding, check out The Project Overview for more details about our approach.

To ensure this research reaches a broader audience, the project employs digital humanities tools, specifically Project Endings static site generation tools, to create a sustainable site that illustrates who was brought into Bartlett’s canon. This sustainable platform provides an enduring resource for exploring the quoted individuals, their origins, and their inclusion across BFQ editions. To learn more about our collaborative effort, meet The Team behind this project.

Through this combination of historical analysis and digital dissemination, the project aims to address how to bridge traditional scholarship with innovative methodologies, inviting reflection on whose voices are remembered and why. For technical insights, visit How Did They Make That for a behind-the-scenes look at our methods and tools.
